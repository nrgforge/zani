# Validating the Palette Collection
*2026-03-07*

## Abstract

This essay documents a source-backed validation of Zani's 40 palette-species assignments. Each palette is named after a Cascadia bioregion species whose real botanical colors should be moderately congruent with the palette's color profile (Invariant 16). The validation compared palette RGB values against documented species colors from the Oregon Flora Project, USDA PLANTS Database, USFS Silvics and FEIS, Burke Herbarium, McCune & Geiser's macrolichen keys, and regional field guides. Of the 40 palettes, 28 scored A (strong match) and 12 scored B (reasonable match). No palette scored C or D. However, two palettes — Oregon Sunshine and Balsamroot — are marginal B's whose species names prime expectations the palettes cannot satisfy, three species have geographic borderline status, and six provenance descriptions contain factual errors. The essay recommends three species replacements (Bracken, Licorice Fern, Silver Fir), one category move (Oregon Sunshine from Light Warm to Light Vivid), one retirement (Columbine, whose hue slot becomes redundant), one species name correction (Jack-o'-Lantern), and six provenance text fixes — maintaining the collection at 40 palettes while raising the projected A-score count from 28 to 32.

## Methodology

### Scoring criteria

Each palette was scored on a 4-point scale for how well its color profile holistically evokes the species' real botanical appearance:

- **A (Strong match):** The palette would be recognized by someone familiar with the species.
- **B (Reasonable match):** A recognizable connection exists, but with drift in hue, tone, or emphasis.
- **C (Weak match):** The connection is a stretch; the species name primes expectations the palette does not satisfy.
- **D (Mismatch):** No recognizable connection between palette colors and species appearance.

Geographic status was assessed against the Cascadia bioregion: coastal British Columbia through northern California, east to the Cascades crest.

### Sources

Primary botanical databases: Oregon Flora Project (oregonflora.org), USDA PLANTS Database (plants.usda.gov), USFS Fire Effects Information System (FEIS), USFS Silvics of North America, Burke Herbarium Image Collection. For lichens: McCune & Geiser's *Macrolichens of the Pacific Northwest* key (lichens.twinferntech.net). For fungi: MycoMatch, regional mycological societies. Secondary sources: Portland Nursery native plant profiles, Native Plants PNW, Washington Native Plant Society, Calflora, iNaturalist occurrence data, Gardenia.net, Wikipedia (for range data cross-referencing).

## Recommended Changes

### Species replacements

Three palettes warrant species replacement. In each case the palette colors are fine — the problem is the species name priming expectations the palette cannot satisfy, or the species not being genuinely Cascadian.

**Balsamroot (#25, Light Warm) → Licorice Fern.** *Polypodium glycyrrhiza* — bright green fronds with golden-brown to cinnamon-brown sori, growing epiphytically on mossy bigleaf maples. The bright green fronds match the Rgb(218, 242, 210) background (more saturated than bracken). The sori age from "buttery yellow" through "rich golden-brown" to "red-brown to cinnamon-brown" (Gardenia.net, UBC Botany), mapping to both warm accent colors. "Licorice" evokes warm, sweet, amber-brown — fitting for Light Warm. Quintessentially Cascadian: the defining PNW epiphytic fern, southern Alaska through coastal CA. Projected score: A.

**Reindeer Lichen (#40, Light Muted) → Silver Fir.** *Abies amabilis* (Pacific Silver Fir) — silvery-white needle undersides from dense stomatal bands, light gray smooth bark, dark green needle tops, and deep purple mature cones. This is the only candidate matching all three of the palette's color families: silvery blue-gray (Rgb(225, 232, 238) background), muted green (heading and emphasis), and muted purple (Rgb(80, 72, 90) link). The species name was already anticipated in ADR-015 as an example two-word palette name. Mid-elevation Cascadia tree, SE Alaska through OR. Projected score: A. Sources: OSU Landscape Plants, USFS Silvics, Conifers.org.

**Oregon Sunshine's vacated Light Warm slot (#22) → Bracken.** *Pteridium aquilinum* (Western Bracken Fern) — characteristically light green fronds (lighter than most PNW ferns), straw-brown stipes, and amber-gold autumn tones. The light green matches the Rgb(230, 242, 205) background. The straw-to-brown stipes map to the warm amber heading. The word "bracken" is itself used as a color name meaning golden-brown in textile/fabric contexts (Woodland Trust). Extremely widespread: Alaska to Baja California, every PNW county, both sides of the Cascades. Projected score: A. Sources: USFS FEIS, WSU PNW Plants, Woodland Trust.

### Category move: Oregon Sunshine

Oregon Sunshine (*Eriophyllum lanatum*) moves from Light Warm to Light Vivid with a redesigned palette.

The species' defining visual — bright golden-yellow daisy-like flowers documented by every source consulted (OSU Extension, Portland Nursery, USDA Fact Sheet) — is high-chroma saturated color. This is definitionally "vivid," not "gentle, morning." The current Light Warm placement forces the palette to suppress the feature Oregon Sunshine is most known for.

Yellow is the one major hue completely absent from Light Vivid. The current five palettes cover red (Paintbrush), orange-red (Columbine), orange (Tiger Lily), magenta-pink (Farewell), and blue-violet (Camas). Oregon Sunshine fills the gap.

The gray-green woolly foliage (documented as "grayish hue" by Portland Nursery, "woolly" by USDA) can inform a warm near-white background or muted green secondary accents, while the dominant accent is the authentic bright golden-yellow.

### Columbine retirement

Adding Oregon Sunshine to Light Vivid creates a 6th palette in the category. While 6 is within the 3–7 acceptable range established in Essay 003, the hue coverage becomes redundant at the warm end: Paintbrush (red), Columbine (orange-red), Tiger Lily (orange), Oregon Sunshine (yellow) — four palettes in the yellow-to-red quarter of the hue wheel.

Columbine (*Aquilegia formosa*) scored A in source validation and is a fine palette. It is retired not for quality but for hue-diversity optimization. Its orange-red heading (Rgb(170, 62, 25)) sits between Paintbrush's red (Rgb(175, 40, 48)) and Tiger Lily's orange (Rgb(168, 78, 10)) — the most crowded region.

Without Columbine, Light Vivid becomes: Paintbrush (red), Tiger Lily (orange), Oregon Sunshine (yellow), Farewell (magenta-pink), Camas (blue-violet). Five palettes distributed cleanly around the hue wheel. The collection stays at 40.
